The 3 A.M. Downtime Drill

When technology fails, patient care cannot wait for recovery.

A ransomware incident can become a clinical operations problem long before systems are restored.

Patient information may become unavailable. Familiar workflows can break down. Clinical teams must coordinate care while working with incomplete information, manual processes, and changing operational conditions.

The question isn't only how quickly technology can be restored. It's what happens to patients while recovery is underway.

A clinical perspective on healthcare cyber resilience

The 3 A.M. Downtime Drill is a fixed-scope, scenario-based clinical ransomware tabletop designed to help hospital teams examine the patient-care implications of technology failure.

Built from emergency department downtime experience, the exercise connects cybersecurity incident scenarios to the realities of clinical operations.

What the exercise examines

Clinical workflow disruption

What happens when essential systems become unavailable?

Incident coordination

How do clinical operations, IT, and cybersecurity teams communicate and make decisions?

Patient safety

Which clinical dependencies and risks require attention during downtime?

Operational continuity

How do teams maintain essential workflows while systems are being restored?

Recovery considerations

What must be addressed beyond technical restoration to support a return to normal operations?

Designed for hospital teams

The Drill is intended for healthcare organizations preparing for ransomware incidents and other technology failures that could disrupt clinical operations.

  • Scoped in advance. Engagement scope, scenarios, participants, and deliverables are agreed upon before the exercise.
  • No patient data. The exercise is designed to work without access to patient data.
